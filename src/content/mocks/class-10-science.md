## Section B | 2 | Very short answer questions.
W[2]: Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction of quicklime with water. Name the type of reaction and state whether heat is absorbed or released.
A: CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + heat. (1)
It is a combination reaction, and it is exothermic (heat is released). (1)

W[2]: Why should curd and other sour substances not be kept in brass or copper vessels?
A: Curd and sour substances contain acids. (½)
These acids react with copper/brass (zinc) to form poisonous, harmful salts that spoil the food and can make a person ill. (1½)

W[2]: Give any two differences between arteries and veins.
A: Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood to the heart. (1)
Arteries have thick, elastic walls because blood is under high pressure; veins have thin walls and valves to prevent backflow. (1)

W[2]: State the role of (i) bile juice and (ii) villi in human digestion.
A: (i) Bile (from the liver) makes the food alkaline so pancreatic enzymes can act, and emulsifies fats into small globules. (1)
(ii) Villi are finger-like projections in the small intestine that increase the surface area for absorption of digested food. (1)

W[2]: The refractive index of glass is 1.5. Find the speed of light in glass. (Speed of light in vacuum = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
A: n = c/v, so v = c/n. (1)
v = (3 × 10⁸)/1.5 = 2 × 10⁸ m/s. (1)

W[2]: Why is the ozone layer important? Name the chemicals mainly responsible for its depletion.
A: The ozone layer in the upper atmosphere absorbs most of the harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun, which can cause skin cancer and harm living beings. (1)
Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), used in refrigerants and fire extinguishers, are mainly responsible for its depletion. (1)

## Section C | 3 | Short answer questions.
W[3]: What are amphoteric oxides? Give one example and write equations showing its reaction with an acid and a base.
A: Metal oxides that react with both acids and bases to give salt and water are amphoteric oxides. (1)
Example: aluminium oxide. Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O. (1)
Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O (sodium aluminate). (1)

W[3]: What is esterification? Write the chemical equation for the reaction of ethanol with ethanoic acid. What happens when the product is treated with sodium hydroxide?
A: The reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst to form a sweet-smelling ester is esterification. (1)
CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O (in presence of conc. H₂SO₄). (1)
With NaOH, the ester is converted back to ethanol and sodium ethanoate: CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → C₂H₅OH + CH₃COONa. This is saponification. (1)

W[3]: What is meant by double circulation? Why is it necessary in human beings?
A: Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle of the body – once through the pulmonary circuit (heart → lungs → heart) and once through the systemic circuit (heart → body → heart). (1½)
It keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate, giving a highly efficient supply of oxygen to the body. (1)
This is needed because birds and mammals are warm-blooded and need a lot of energy to maintain body temperature. (½)

W[3]: Mendel crossed pure tall pea plants (TT) with pure short pea plants (tt). What did he observe in the F₁ and F₂ generations? What conclusion did he draw?
A: F₁: all plants were tall (Tt). (1)
F₂ (selfing F₁): tall and short plants in the ratio 3 : 1; genotypes TT : Tt : tt = 1 : 2 : 1. (1)
Conclusion: traits are controlled by factors (genes) present in pairs; tallness is dominant and shortness is recessive; the recessive trait reappears in F₂, so factors are not lost. (1)

W[3]: An object is placed 20 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 15 cm. Find the position and nature of the image and its magnification.
A: u = −20 cm, f = +15 cm. Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/v = 1/15 − 1/20 = 1/60. (1)
v = +60 cm, so the image is formed 60 cm on the other side of the lens. (1)
m = v/u = 60/(−20) = −3. The image is real, inverted and three times magnified. (1)

W[3]: Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in parallel to a 6 V battery. Find (i) the equivalent resistance (ii) the total current (iii) the current through the 2 Ω resistor.
A: (i) 1/Rp = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = (3 + 2 + 1)/6 = 1, so Rp = 1 Ω. (1)
(ii) I = V/R = 6/1 = 6 A. (1)
(iii) Voltage across each resistor is 6 V, so I₂Ω = 6/2 = 3 A. (1)

W[3]: List any three properties of magnetic field lines.
A: Any three, 1 mark each: They emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole outside the magnet (and go from south to north inside, forming closed loops). No two field lines ever intersect. The field is stronger where lines are closer together. The tangent at any point gives the direction of the field.

## Section D | 5 | Long answer questions.
W[5]: What is the chlor-alkali process? Write the balanced equation, name the three products, state where each is formed and give one use of each.
A: When electricity is passed through brine (aqueous sodium chloride), it decomposes to form sodium hydroxide; the process is called the chlor-alkali process (chlor for chlorine, alkali for NaOH). (1)
2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + Cl₂(g) + H₂(g). (1)
Chlorine gas is given off at the anode; hydrogen gas at the cathode; sodium hydroxide solution forms near the cathode. (1)
Uses: H₂ – fuels, margarine, ammonia for fertilisers; Cl₂ – water treatment, PVC, disinfectants, CFCs; NaOH – soaps and detergents, paper making, degreasing metals. (2)

W[5]: Describe the process of fertilisation in flowering plants. What changes take place in the flower after fertilisation?
A: Pollination: pollen grains are transferred from the anther to the stigma (self or cross pollination by wind, water, insects). (1)
A pollen tube grows out of the pollen grain, travels through the style and reaches the ovule in the ovary. (1)
The male germ cell fuses with the female germ cell (egg) in the ovule to form a zygote – this is fertilisation. (1)
After fertilisation: the zygote divides to form an embryo; the ovule develops a tough coat and becomes the seed. (1)
The ovary grows rapidly and ripens to form the fruit; petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma may shrivel and fall off. (1)

W[5]: What is myopia? State two causes. How is it corrected? A myopic person cannot see objects clearly beyond 80 cm. Find the nature and power of the lens needed.
A: Myopia (near-sightedness): a person can see nearby objects clearly but not distant objects; the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina. (1)
Causes: excessive curvature of the eye lens; elongation of the eyeball. (1)
Correction: a concave (diverging) lens of suitable power, which brings the image back onto the retina. (1)
The lens must form the image of an object at infinity at the far point, so f = −80 cm = −0.8 m. (1)
P = 1/f = 1/(−0.8) = −1.25 D; a concave lens of power −1.25 D. (1)

## Section E | 4 | Case-study questions (1 + 1 + 2 marks).
W[4]: Case study — The baker's secret. A baker adds a white powder to cake batter. When the cake is baked, it becomes soft and spongy. The same powder is also used in antacids. When this powder is heated strongly, it gives a substance used to make washing soda.
(i) Give the chemical name and formula of the powder. (1)
(ii) Why does the cake become soft and spongy? (1)
(iii) Write the equation for the effect of heat on the powder, and how washing soda is obtained from the product. (2)
A: (i) Sodium hydrogencarbonate, NaHCO₃ (baking soda). (1)
(ii) On heating, it releases carbon dioxide gas which gets trapped in the batter and makes it rise. (1)
(iii) 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ (on heating). (1)
Recrystallising sodium carbonate gives washing soda: Na₂CO₃ + 10H₂O → Na₂CO₃·10H₂O. (1)

W[4]: Case study — Chemical messengers. A doctor tells a patient with a swollen neck to use iodised salt. Another patient has high blood sugar and is advised insulin injections. A third person notices that when frightened, his heart beats fast and he breathes faster.
(i) Why is iodised salt advised for the first patient? (1)
(ii) Which gland secretes insulin and which disease results from its deficiency? (1)
(iii) Name the hormone responsible for the third person's response, its gland, and two of its effects. (2)
A: (i) Iodine is needed by the thyroid gland to make thyroxine; its deficiency causes goitre (swollen neck). (1)
(ii) The pancreas secretes insulin; its deficiency causes diabetes. (1)
(iii) Adrenaline, secreted by the adrenal glands. (1)
Effects (any two): faster heartbeat to supply more oxygen to muscles; increased breathing rate; blood diverted to skeletal muscles; preparing the body for "fight or flight". (1)

W[4]: Case study — Electricity bill. A house uses four bulbs each rated 100 W, 220 V. Each bulb is used for 5 hours daily.
(i) Find the current drawn by one bulb. (1)
(ii) Find the resistance of the filament of one bulb. (1)
(iii) Find the electrical energy consumed by the four bulbs in 30 days, and its cost at ₹5 per unit. (2)
A: (i) I = P/V = 100/220 ≈ 0.45 A. (1)
(ii) R = V²/P = (220 × 220)/100 = 484 Ω. (1)
(iii) Energy = 4 × 100 W × 5 h × 30 = 60,000 Wh = 60 kWh = 60 units. (1)
Cost = 60 × ₹5 = ₹300. (1)
