W[2]: Factorise 12x² − 7x + 1 by splitting the middle term.
A: Product ac = 12; split −7x as −4x − 3x: 12x² − 4x − 3x + 1. (1)
= 4x(3x − 1) − 1(3x − 1) = (3x − 1)(4x − 1). (1)

W[2]: Two lines intersect at a point O. One of the angles formed is 40°. Find the other three angles, giving reasons.
A: The angle vertically opposite to 40° is 40° (vertically opposite angles are equal). (1)
The angles adjacent to it form linear pairs, so each is 180° − 40° = 140°. The angles are 40°, 140°, 40°, 140°. (1)

W[2]: Find the curved surface area of a right circular cone whose slant height is 10 cm and base radius is 7 cm. (π = 22/7)
A: CSA = πrl. (1)
= (22/7) × 7 × 10 = 220 cm². (1)

W[2]: Check which of the following are solutions of x + 2y = 4: (i) (0, 2) (ii) (2, 0) (iii) (4, 1).
A: (i) 0 + 2(2) = 4 ✓ – a solution. (½)
(ii) 2 + 2(0) = 2 ≠ 4 – not a solution. (½)
(iii) 4 + 2(1) = 6 ≠ 4 – not a solution. (1)

## Section C | 3 | Short answer questions.
W[3]: If x = 3 + 2√2, find the values of x + 1/x and x² + 1/x².
A: 1/x = 1/(3 + 2√2) = (3 − 2√2)/(9 − 8) = 3 − 2√2. (1)
x + 1/x = (3 + 2√2) + (3 − 2√2) = 6. (1)
x² + 1/x² = (x + 1/x)² − 2 = 36 − 2 = 34. (1)

W[3]: Factorise x³ − 23x² + 142x − 120.
A: p(1) = 1 − 23 + 142 − 120 = 0, so (x − 1) is a factor (Factor Theorem). (1)
Dividing, x³ − 23x² + 142x − 120 = (x − 1)(x² − 22x + 120). (1)
x² − 22x + 120 = (x − 10)(x − 12). So the polynomial = (x − 1)(x − 10)(x − 12). (1)

W[3]: Prove that the angles opposite to equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal.
A: Given: △ABC with AB = AC. To prove: ∠B = ∠C. Construction: draw AD, the bisector of ∠A, meeting BC at D. (1)
In △BAD and △CAD: AB = AC (given), ∠BAD = ∠CAD (construction), AD = AD (common). So △BAD ≅ △CAD (SAS). (1½)
Hence ∠ABD = ∠ACD (CPCT), i.e. ∠B = ∠C. (½)

W[3]: ABCD is a rhombus and P, Q, R, S are the mid-points of AB, BC, CD and DA respectively. Show that PQRS is a rectangle.
A: In △ABC, P and Q are mid-points of AB and BC, so PQ ∥ AC and PQ = ½AC (Mid-point Theorem). Similarly, in △ADC, SR ∥ AC and SR = ½AC. So PQ ∥ SR and PQ = SR, hence PQRS is a parallelogram. (1½)
Similarly, in △ABD, PS ∥ BD. (½)
Diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular, so AC ⟂ BD. Since PQ ∥ AC and PS ∥ BD, PQ ⟂ PS, i.e. ∠QPS = 90°. A parallelogram with one right angle is a rectangle. (1)

W[3]: Find the area of a triangle whose sides are 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm using Heron's formula.
A: s = (13 + 14 + 15)/2 = 21 cm. (1)
Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)] = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6). (1)
= √7056 = 84 cm². (1)

W[3]: A hemispherical bowl has an inner radius of 3.5 cm. How much water (in litres) can it hold? (π = 22/7)
A: Volume = (2/3)πr³ = (2/3) × (22/7) × 3.5 × 3.5 × 3.5. (1)
= (2/3) × 134.75 ≈ 89.83 cm³. (1½)
Since 1000 cm³ = 1 litre, it holds about 0.0898 litre. (½)

## Section D | 5 | Long answer questions.
W[5]: (i) Factorise 27x³ + y³ + z³ − 9xyz. (ii) Without actually calculating the cubes, find the value of (−12)³ + 7³ + 5³. (iii) Expand (2x − y + z)².
A: (i) Using a³ + b³ + c³ − 3abc = (a + b + c)(a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca) with a = 3x, b = y, c = z: (1)
27x³ + y³ + z³ − 9xyz = (3x + y + z)(9x² + y² + z² − 3xy − yz − 3zx). (1)
(ii) Since −12 + 7 + 5 = 0, a³ + b³ + c³ = 3abc. (1)
Value = 3 × (−12) × 7 × 5 = −1260. (½)
(iii) (2x − y + z)² = 4x² + y² + z² − 4xy − 2yz + 4zx. (1½)

W[5]: Prove that the sum of the angles of a triangle is 180°. In △ABC, the angles A, B and C are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find all the angles and the exterior angle at C.
A: Given △PQR. Through P draw a line XPY parallel to QR. (1)
∠XPQ = ∠PQR and ∠YPR = ∠PRQ (alternate interior angles). (1)
∠XPQ + ∠QPR + ∠YPR = 180° (angles on a straight line), so ∠Q + ∠P + ∠R = 180°. (1)
2k + 3k + 4k = 180° ⇒ k = 20°, so ∠A = 40°, ∠B = 60°, ∠C = 80°. (1)
Exterior angle at C = 180° − 80° = 100° (= ∠A + ∠B). (1)

W[5]: Prove that equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre. Also, a chord of length 16 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the distance of the chord from the centre.
A: Given: AB = CD are chords of a circle with centre O. To prove: ∠AOB = ∠COD. (½)
In △AOB and △COD: OA = OC, OB = OD (radii) and AB = CD (given). So △AOB ≅ △COD (SSS). (1½)
Hence ∠AOB = ∠COD (CPCT). (1)
The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so half-chord = 8 cm. (1)
Distance = √(10² − 8²) = √36 = 6 cm. (1)

W[5]: A conical tent is 10 m high and the radius of its base is 24 m. Find (i) the slant height (ii) the cost of canvas required to make it at ₹70 per m² (iii) the volume of air inside the tent. (π = 22/7)
A: (i) l = √(h² + r²) = √(100 + 576) = √676 = 26 m. (1)
(ii) Canvas = CSA = πrl = (22/7) × 24 × 26 = 13728/7 m². (1)
Cost = (13728/7) × 70 = ₹1,37,280. (1)
(iii) Volume = (1/3)πr²h = (1/3) × (22/7) × 576 × 10 ≈ 6034.29 m³. (2)

## Section E | 4 | Case-study questions (1 + 1 + 2 marks).
W[4]: Case study — The school garden. A gardener marks four trees on a grid with the school gate as the origin: A(2, 3), B(−4, 2), C(−3, −5) and D(5, −1).
(i) In which quadrant does tree C lie? (1)
(ii) What is the abscissa of tree B? (1)
(iii) A tap is on the x-axis, 3 units to the left of the gate. Write its coordinates. In which quadrant does tree D lie? (2)
A: (i) Both coordinates of C are negative, so it lies in quadrant III. (1)
(ii) Abscissa (x-coordinate) of B = −4. (1)
(iii) Tap = (−3, 0). (1) D(5, −1) has x positive, y negative, so it lies in quadrant IV. (1)

W[4]: Case study — Taxi fare. In a city, the taxi fare is ₹8 for the first kilometre and ₹5 for each subsequent kilometre. Let x be the distance covered (in km) and y the total fare (in ₹).
(i) Write a linear equation for this information. (1)
(ii) Find the fare for a 10 km ride. (1)
(iii) For what distance is the fare ₹78? Write any two points that lie on the graph of this equation. (2)
A: (i) y = 8 + 5(x − 1), i.e. y = 5x + 3 (or 5x − y + 3 = 0). (1)
(ii) y = 5(10) + 3 = ₹53. (1)
(iii) 5x + 3 = 78 ⇒ x = 15 km. (1) Points: (1, 8) and (2, 13) (any two correct points). (1)

W[4]: Case study — Test marks. The marks (out of 50) of 40 students are: 0–10: 3, 10–20: 7, 20–30: 12, 30–40: 10, 40–50: 8.
(i) Find the class mark of the class 20–30. (1)
(ii) Which class has the maximum number of students? (1)
(iii) How many students scored less than 30 marks? What percentage of students scored 40 or more? (2)
A: (i) (20 + 30)/2 = 25. (1)
(ii) 20–30 (12 students). (1)
(iii) Less than 30: 3 + 7 + 12 = 22 students. (1) 40 or more: 8 students = (8/40) × 100 = 20%. (1)
