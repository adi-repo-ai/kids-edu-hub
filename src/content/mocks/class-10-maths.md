## Section A | 1 | Multiple-choice questions (Q1–18) and assertion–reason questions (Q19–20). Choose the correct option.
Q: The HCF of 96 and 404 is
* 4
- 8
- 12
- 2
> 96 = 2⁵ × 3 and 404 = 2² × 101. The common prime is 2 with smallest power 2², so HCF = 4.

Q: If one zero of 2x² − 3x + k is the reciprocal of the other, then k equals
* 2
- −2
- 1/2
- 3
> Product of zeroes = k/2. If the zeroes are α and 1/α, the product is 1, so k/2 = 1 and k = 2.

Q: The pair of equations 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 15 has
- a unique solution
- infinitely many solutions
* no solution
- exactly two solutions
> a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = 1/2 but c₁/c₂ = 1/3. The lines are parallel, so there is no solution.

Q: The quadratic equation 2x² − 4x + 3 = 0 has
- two distinct real roots
- two equal real roots
* no real roots
- more than two real roots
> D = b² − 4ac = 16 − 24 = −8 < 0, so there are no real roots.

## Section B | 2 | Very short answer questions.
W[2]: Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational.
A: Assume 3 + 2√5 = a/b where a, b are co-prime integers, b ≠ 0. (½)
Then √5 = (a − 3b)/(2b), which is rational. (1)
This contradicts the fact that √5 is irrational, so 3 + 2√5 is irrational. (½)

W[2]: Find the zeroes of the polynomial x² − 3 and verify the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients.
A: x² − 3 = (x − √3)(x + √3), so the zeroes are √3 and −√3. (1)
Sum = 0 = −b/a = −0/1 ✓. Product = −3 = c/a = −3/1 ✓. (1)

W[2]: In △ABC, DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC. If AD/DB = 3/5 and AC = 5.6 cm, find AE.
A: By the Basic Proportionality Theorem, AD/DB = AE/EC = 3/5. (1)
So AE = (3/8) × AC = (3/8) × 5.6 = 2.1 cm. (1)

W[2]: If tan A = cot B, prove that A + B = 90° (A and B acute).
A: cot B = tan(90° − B). (1)
So tan A = tan(90° − B), giving A = 90° − B, i.e. A + B = 90°. (1)

W[2]: Find the area of a quadrant of a circle whose circumference is 22 cm.
A: 2πr = 22 ⇒ r = 22 × 7 / 44 = 3.5 cm. (1)
Area of quadrant = πr²/4 = (22/7 × 3.5 × 3.5)/4 = 38.5/4 = 9.625 cm². (1)

## Section C | 3 | Short answer questions.
W[3]: Find the HCF and LCM of 6, 72 and 120 using prime factorisation.
A: 6 = 2 × 3, 72 = 2³ × 3², 120 = 2³ × 3 × 5. (1)
HCF = 2 × 3 = 6. (1)
LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 = 360. (1)

W[3]: Solve 2x + 3y = 11 and 2x − 4y = −24, and hence find m for which y = mx + 3.
A: Subtracting the equations: 7y = 35, so y = 5. (1)
Then 2x = 11 − 15 = −4, so x = −2. (1)
5 = m(−2) + 3 ⇒ m = −1. (1)

W[3]: Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
A: Let PA and PB be tangents from P to a circle with centre O. Join OA, OB, OP. (½)
OA ⟂ PA and OB ⟂ PB (tangent ⟂ radius), so ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90°. (1)
In △OAP and △OBP: OA = OB (radii), OP is common, so the triangles are congruent by RHS. (1)
Hence PA = PB (CPCT). (½)

W[3]: Prove that (sin A + cosec A)² + (cos A + sec A)² = 7 + tan²A + cot²A.
A: LHS = sin²A + cosec²A + 2 + cos²A + sec²A + 2. (1)
= (sin²A + cos²A) + 4 + (1 + cot²A) + (1 + tan²A). (1)
= 1 + 4 + 2 + tan²A + cot²A = 7 + tan²A + cot²A = RHS. (1)

W[3]: Find the ratio in which the y-axis divides the line segment joining (5, −6) and (−1, −4). Also find the point of division.
A: Let the ratio be k : 1. x-coordinate = (−k + 5)/(k + 1) = 0 ⇒ k = 5, so the ratio is 5 : 1. (2)
y = (5 × (−4) + 1 × (−6))/6 = −26/6 = −13/3. Point = (0, −13/3). (1)

W[3]: Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that (i) the sum is 8 (ii) a doublet appears.
A: Total outcomes = 36. (½)
(i) Sum 8: (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) ⇒ P = 5/36. (1½)
(ii) Doublets: 6 outcomes ⇒ P = 6/36 = 1/6. (1)

## Section D | 5 | Long answer questions.
W[5]: A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less. Find the speed of the train.
A: Let the speed be x km/h. 360/x − 360/(x + 5) = 1. (1)
360 × 5 = x(x + 5) ⇒ x² + 5x − 1800 = 0. (2)
(x + 45)(x − 40) = 0 ⇒ x = 40 (speed cannot be negative). (1)
Speed = 40 km/h. (1)

W[5]: State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem.
A: Statement: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle intersecting the other two sides at distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio. (1)
Given △ABC with DE ∥ BC. To prove AD/DB = AE/EC. Join BE and CD; draw EN ⟂ AB and DM ⟂ AC. (1)
ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB and ar(ADE)/ar(DEC) = AE/EC (same heights). (1½)
ar(BDE) = ar(DEC) (same base DE, between the same parallels). (1)
Hence AD/DB = AE/EC. (½)

W[5]: From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Find the height of the tower.
A: Let the distance between building and tower be x. Angle of depression 45° ⇒ tan 45° = 7/x ⇒ x = 7 m. (2)
Height of tower above the building's top = x tan 60° = 7√3 m. (2)
Height of tower = 7 + 7√3 = 7(1 + √3) ≈ 19.12 m. (1)

W[5]: A solid toy is a hemisphere surmounted by a right circular cone of the same radius 3.5 cm. The total height of the toy is 15.5 cm. Find its total surface area. (π = 22/7)
A: Height of cone = 15.5 − 3.5 = 12 cm. (1)
Slant height l = √(12² + 3.5²) = √156.25 = 12.5 cm. (1)
TSA = CSA of hemisphere + CSA of cone = 2πr² + πrl = πr(2r + l). (1)
= (22/7) × 3.5 × (7 + 12.5) = 11 × 19.5 = 214.5 cm². (2)

## Section E | 4 | Case-study questions (1 + 1 + 2 marks).
W[4]: Case study — Auditorium seating. A school auditorium has 20 seats in the first row, 22 in the second, 24 in the third, and so on.
(i) How many seats are in the 10th row? (1)
(ii) If there are 30 rows, how many seats are in the last row? (1)
(iii) Find the total number of seats in 30 rows. (2)
A: The seats form an AP with a = 20, d = 2.
(i) a₁₀ = 20 + 9 × 2 = 38. (1)
(ii) a₃₀ = 20 + 29 × 2 = 78. (1)
(iii) S₃₀ = 30/2 × (20 + 78) = 15 × 98 = 1470 seats. (2)

W[4]: Case study — Three friends' houses. On a map, Asha's house is at A(2, 1) and Bharat's house is at B(6, 4) (units in km).
(i) Find the distance between the two houses. (1)
(ii) A shop is exactly midway between them. Find its coordinates. (1)
(iii) A bus stop divides AB in the ratio 1 : 3. Find its coordinates. (2)
A: (i) AB = √(4² + 3²) = 5 km. (1)
(ii) Midpoint = (4, 2.5). (1)
(iii) Using the section formula with m : n = 1 : 3: x = (1 × 6 + 3 × 2)/4 = 3, y = (1 × 4 + 3 × 1)/4 = 7/4. Bus stop = (3, 7/4). (2)

W[4]: Case study — Pocket money survey. The daily pocket money (₹) of 50 students: 0–20: 5, 20–40: 10, 40–60: 18, 60–80: 12, 80–100: 5.
(i) Find the modal class. (1)
(ii) Find the median class. (1)
(iii) Find the mean pocket money. (2)
A: (i) Highest frequency 18 ⇒ modal class 40–60. (1)
(ii) n/2 = 25. Cumulative frequencies: 5, 15, 33 … ⇒ median class 40–60. (1)
(iii) Σfx = 10×5 + 30×10 + 50×18 + 70×12 + 90×5 = 50 + 300 + 900 + 840 + 450 = 2540. Mean = 2540/50 = ₹50.80. (2)
