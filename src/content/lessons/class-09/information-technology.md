=== communication-skills ===
## The big idea
**Communication** comes from the Latin word *communicare*, meaning "to share". It is the process of exchanging information, ideas and feelings so that the other person understands them. Whether you are greeting a teacher, answering a phone call or writing an e-mail, the way you communicate shapes how people see you.

> [!KEY] The communication cycle
> **Sender** (has an idea) → **Message** → **Channel** (speech, letter, e-mail, phone) → **Receiver** → **Feedback**. Without feedback, the sender cannot be sure the message was understood.

## Elements of communication
- **Sender** – the person who starts the communication.
- **Message** – the idea, information or feeling being shared.
- **Channel/medium** – the way it is sent (face to face, phone, letter, e-mail, video call).
- **Receiver** – the person who gets the message.
- **Feedback** – the receiver's response (a reply, a nod, a question).

Feedback should be **specific**, **timely**, **polite** and **helpful**. It can be **positive** ("Your project was well organised") or **negative/constructive** ("Next time add a conclusion").

## Methods of communication
| Method | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verbal – oral** | Spoken words | Talking, phone call, speech, video meeting |
| **Verbal – written** | Words in writing | Letters, e-mails, SMS, notices, reports |
| **Non-verbal** | Without words | Facial expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, touch, personal space, tone of voice |
| **Visual** | Images and signs | Traffic signs, charts, maps, emojis, posters |

> [!EXAMPLE] Reading body language
> Smiling and nodding → interest and agreement. Arms crossed and looking away → disagreement or discomfort. Keep an open posture and steady eye contact when you speak.

> [!MISTAKE] Silence still communicates
> Not replying, or looking bored, sends a message too. Non-verbal signals should match your words.

## Basic greetings and introductions
Greetings set the tone of any conversation.
- **Formal greetings** (teachers, elders, at work): "Good morning, Sir/Ma'am", "Namaste", "How do you do?".
- **Informal greetings** (friends, family): "Hi!", "Hello!", "What's up?".
- Time-based greetings: **Good morning** (till noon), **Good afternoon** (noon to about 5 pm), **Good evening** (after that). **Good night** is used only when leaving or going to bed, not as a greeting.

> [!EXAMPLE] Introducing yourself
> "Good morning, everyone. My name is Arjun Mehta. I study in Class 9 at Green Valley School. I enjoy coding and playing football. Thank you."
> Introducing others: "Ma'am, this is my friend Priya. She is the captain of our quiz team."

## Pronunciation and speaking well
Speak clearly, at a moderate pace, with correct **pronunciation** (the way a word is spoken). Practise by listening to news readers, reading aloud and recording yourself. Use polite words: **please, thank you, sorry, excuse me**.

## Writing basics
Writing needs correct **capitalisation** (first word of a sentence, names, "I"), **punctuation** (full stop, comma, question mark, apostrophe, exclamation mark) and the basic parts of speech. A good sentence has a **subject** and a **verb** (e.g. *Meera writes*).

> [!TIP] Exam tip
> In "write a self-introduction" questions, include greeting, name, class/school, interests and a closing line.

## Quick recap
- Communication cycle: sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback.
- Methods: verbal (oral, written), non-verbal, visual.
- Formal vs informal greetings; good night is not a greeting.
- Speak clearly, use polite words, and use correct capitalisation and punctuation.

=== self-management-skills ===
## The big idea
**Self-management** is the ability to control your own thoughts, emotions, behaviour and time so you can reach your goals. A self-managed person is confident, well-groomed, organised and positive. In Class 9 you study **personality**, **self-confidence**, **positive thinking** and **personal grooming and hygiene**.

## Self-management skills
Important self-management skills include:
- **Self-awareness** – knowing your strengths and weaknesses.
- **Self-control** – managing emotions like anger.
- **Self-confidence** – believing in your abilities.
- **Problem solving**, **self-motivation**, **personal hygiene**, **positive thinking**, **team work** and **time management**.

## Personality
**Personality** is the set of qualities (thoughts, feelings, behaviour) that make a person unique. It is shaped by **heredity, environment, family, friends, school and culture**.

> [!KEY] The Big Five personality traits (OCEAN)
> - **O**penness – curious, likes new ideas.
> - **C**onscientiousness – organised, responsible.
> - **E**xtraversion – outgoing, energetic.
> - **A**greeableness – friendly, kind, cooperative.
> - **N**euroticism – tends to feel anxious or moody (the opposite is emotional stability).

Some common problems that hold people back are **shyness**, **fear**, **low confidence** and **anxiety**. These can be reduced with practice, support and positive thinking.

## Self-confidence
**Self-confidence** is trust in your own abilities and judgement. Confident people speak up, try new things and accept mistakes.

Steps to build self-confidence:
1. **Know your strengths** and list your achievements.
2. **Set small, achievable goals** and complete them.
3. **Think positive** and stop comparing yourself with others.
4. **Prepare well** before tests, speeches or interviews.
5. **Learn from mistakes** instead of fearing them.
6. Stay **well-groomed** – looking neat helps you feel confident.

> [!MISTAKE] Confidence is not overconfidence
> Confidence comes with preparation and respect for others. Overconfidence ignores preparation and often leads to failure.

## Positive thinking
Positive thinking means expecting good results and seeing problems as chances to learn. Tips: start the day with something you are grateful for, stay with positive people, replace "I can't" with "I'll try", and take care of your health.

## Personal grooming and hygiene
**Grooming** means keeping yourself neat and presentable: clean, ironed clothes, polished shoes, tidy hair, trimmed nails.
**Personal hygiene** keeps you healthy and confident:
- Bathe daily and wash hands before eating and after using the toilet.
- Brush teeth twice a day.
- Wear clean clothes and socks.
- Cover your mouth while coughing or sneezing.

Healthy living also means **exercise**, a **balanced diet** and **8 hours of sleep**.

> [!TIP] Remember the three steps of hygiene
> Care (keep your body clean), Wash (hands, clothes, body) and Avoid (germs, sharing personal items like towels and combs).

## Quick recap
- Self-management = controlling your thoughts, feelings, behaviour and time.
- Personality: unique qualities; Big Five = OCEAN.
- Build confidence through strengths, small goals, preparation and positive thinking.
- Grooming and hygiene boost health and confidence.

=== ict-skills ===
## The big idea
**ICT** stands for **Information and Communication Technology** – all the devices and tools we use to create, store, share and communicate information: computers, smartphones, tablets, the Internet, e-mail and more. This chapter covers the parts of a computer, starting and shutting it down, using the keyboard and mouse, and managing **files and folders**.

## ICT tools
- **Smartphones and tablets** – touch-screen devices for calls, apps, Internet.
- **Computers (desktop/laptop)** – for typing, calculations, presentations, coding.
- **Internet, e-mail, video calls, social media** – for communication.

## Parts of a computer
| Part | Type | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard, mouse, scanner, microphone | **Input devices** | Enter data and commands |
| **CPU (Central Processing Unit)** | Processing | The "brain" – processes instructions |
| Monitor, printer, speakers | **Output devices** | Show or give results |
| RAM, hard disk, pen drive | **Memory/storage** | Store data temporarily or permanently |

**Hardware** = physical parts you can touch. **Software** = programs (e.g. Windows, Ubuntu, LibreOffice).

## Basic computer operations
- **Starting (booting)**: press the power button; the computer runs a **POST (Power-On Self-Test)**, then loads the **operating system**. Log in with your username and password.
- **Desktop**: the first screen, with icons, taskbar/panel and a start menu or app launcher.
- **Shutting down**: save your work, close programs, then **Start > Power > Shut down** (Windows) or the system menu → **Power Off** (Ubuntu). Never just switch off the main supply.

## Using the mouse and keyboard
- **Click** (left button once) – select. **Double-click** – open. **Right-click** – open the context menu. **Drag and drop** – hold the left button to move an item. **Scroll** – use the wheel.
- Keyboard keys: **Enter** (confirm/new line), **Backspace** (delete left), **Delete** (delete right/selected), **Shift** (capitals), **Caps Lock**, **Tab**, **Spacebar**, **arrow keys**, and **Ctrl/Alt** for shortcuts.

## Files and folders
A **file** is a named collection of data (a document, image, song). Its **extension** tells its type: `.odt`, `.txt`, `.jpg`, `.mp3`, `.pdf`. A **folder** (directory) holds files and other folders (sub-folders), like a cupboard with drawers.

> [!EXAMPLE] Managing files (Ubuntu Files / Windows File Explorer)
> - **Create a folder**: open a location → right-click empty space → **New Folder** → type name → Enter.
> - **Rename**: select → **F2** (or right-click → Rename).
> - **Copy**: select → **Ctrl+C** → open destination → **Ctrl+V**.
> - **Move**: **Ctrl+X** then **Ctrl+V** (or drag to another folder).
> - **Delete**: select → **Delete** (goes to **Trash/Recycle Bin**, can be restored).
> - **Search**: type the name in the search box.

> [!MISTAKE] Copy vs move
> Copy keeps the original and makes a duplicate. Move (cut-paste) removes it from the old place.

## Internet and e-mail basics
A **web browser** (Firefox, Chrome) opens websites using their address (**URL**). A **search engine** (Google, Bing) helps find information. An **e-mail address** looks like `name@domain.com`; an e-mail has **To, Cc, Bcc, Subject, Body** and can carry **attachments**.

> [!TIP] Save often
> Press **Ctrl+S** every few minutes. Power cuts do not wait for you to finish!

## Quick recap
- ICT = devices and tools to handle and share information.
- Input → CPU → output; storage keeps data.
- Boot, log in, use the desktop, shut down properly.
- Mouse: click, double-click, right-click, drag. Files have extensions; folders organise them.
- Ctrl+C copy, Ctrl+X cut, Ctrl+V paste, F2 rename.

=== entrepreneurial-skills ===
## The big idea
Have you noticed the local tea stall, the bakery or the mobile repair shop near your home? Each was started by someone who saw a need and took a risk. That person is an **entrepreneur**, their business is an **enterprise**, and the process is **entrepreneurship**.

> [!KEY] Definitions
> - **Entrepreneur**: a person who starts a business, takes the risk and earns profit (or bears losses).
> - **Enterprise**: the business itself.
> - **Entrepreneurship**: the process of identifying an opportunity and creating a business to meet a need.

## Entrepreneurship and the economy
Entrepreneurs:
- produce goods and services people need;
- **create jobs** for themselves and others;
- **use local resources** and skills;
- **increase income** of families and the nation;
- bring **new ideas** (innovation) into the market.

## Types of business activities
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product business** | Makes or sells physical goods | Bakery, furniture making, clothes store |
| **Service business** | Provides a service, not a product | Salon, tuition centre, repair shop, courier |
| **Hybrid business** | Both product and service | Restaurant (cooks and serves food), mobile shop with repairs |

## Business cycle (basic)
Every business follows a simple cycle:
1. **Identify a need** or idea.
2. **Buy raw materials** or arrange resources.
3. **Make the product / prepare the service**.
4. **Sell** to customers.
5. **Earn profit** and reinvest in the business.

> [!EXAMPLE] A small entrepreneur
> Sunita noticed office workers in her area had no healthy lunch option. She started a home tiffin service with ₹5,000 savings. She bought vegetables (raw material), cooked meals (production), delivered them (selling) and used her profit to hire a helper. She is an entrepreneur running a **hybrid** business.

## Qualities of an entrepreneur
- **Hard-working** and **patient**
- **Confident** and **positive**
- **Creative** – thinks of new ideas
- **Risk-taker** – ready to invest time and money
- **Good decision-maker** and **problem-solver**
- **Good communicator** – deals well with customers and suppliers
- **Persistent** – does not give up

> [!MISTAKE] Entrepreneur vs employee
> An **employee** works for someone else and gets a fixed salary. An **entrepreneur** owns the business, earns profit and bears the risk of loss.

## Roles and functions
An entrepreneur plans the business, arranges money and materials, hires workers, decides prices, markets the product and handles problems.

> [!TIP] Remember with examples
> For each type of business, learn two examples. "Name the type of business" questions are common 1-markers.

## Quick recap
- Entrepreneur = risk-taker who starts a business; enterprise = the business; entrepreneurship = the process.
- Entrepreneurs create jobs, products and income.
- Product, service and hybrid businesses.
- Business cycle: need → resources → production → sale → profit.
- Qualities: hard work, confidence, creativity, risk-taking, persistence.

=== green-skills ===
## The big idea
Everything around us – air, water, soil, plants, animals – forms our **environment**. Humans depend on it for food, water and air, yet our activities often damage it. **Green skills** are the knowledge and habits that help us protect the environment while living and working.

## Environment and ecosystem
- **Environment**: all living and non-living things around us and their interactions.
- **Ecosystem**: a community of living organisms interacting with each other and with their non-living surroundings in a particular area, e.g. a pond, a forest, a desert.

> [!KEY] Components of an ecosystem
> - **Biotic (living)**: producers (green plants), consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores) and decomposers (bacteria, fungi).
> - **Abiotic (non-living)**: sunlight, air, water, soil, temperature, minerals.

## Food chain and energy flow
A **food chain** shows who eats whom: **Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle**. Energy flows from the Sun to producers and then to consumers. **Decomposers** break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil, so materials are recycled. Many inter-connected food chains form a **food web**.

## Natural resources
| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Renewable** | Replenished naturally | Sunlight, wind, water, forests (if used wisely) |
| **Non-renewable** | Limited; take millions of years to form | Coal, petroleum, natural gas, minerals |

## Threats to the environment
- **Pollution** – air (vehicle and factory smoke), water (sewage, chemicals), soil (pesticides, plastic), noise.
- **Deforestation** – loss of forests and wildlife habitat.
- **Overuse of resources** – groundwater depletion, overfishing.
- **Global warming and climate change** – caused by greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide and methane.

> [!MISTAKE] Ozone hole ≠ global warming
> Ozone depletion (caused mainly by CFCs) lets more UV rays reach Earth. Global warming is heating of Earth due to greenhouse gases. They are different problems.

## Conservation of natural resources
- **3Rs**: **Reduce** use, **Reuse** items, **Recycle** waste.
- Save electricity and water; use solar energy.
- Plant trees; protect forests and wildlife.
- Rainwater harvesting.
- Use public transport, cycle or walk.
- Segregate wet and dry waste; compost kitchen waste.

## Green economy and green jobs
A **green economy** aims at growth with low carbon emissions, efficient use of resources and social inclusion. **Green jobs** protect or restore the environment, in sectors like renewable energy, organic farming, waste management, water conservation, green construction and eco-tourism.

> [!EXAMPLE] Green jobs around you
> Solar panel installer, organic farmer, waste recycler, rainwater harvesting technician, forest guard.

> [!TIP] Exam tip
> A typical 2-mark question: "Differentiate between biotic and abiotic components with two examples each." Learn clear examples.

## Quick recap
- Environment = all surroundings; ecosystem = living + non-living interacting in an area.
- Biotic: producers, consumers, decomposers. Abiotic: sun, air, water, soil.
- Renewable vs non-renewable resources.
- Threats: pollution, deforestation, global warming. Remedies: 3Rs, saving energy and water, planting trees.
- Green economy and green jobs protect the environment.

=== introduction-to-it-ites-industry ===
## The big idea
**IT (Information Technology)** is the use of computers, software and networks to store, process and send information. **ITeS (IT-enabled Services)** are services delivered using IT, such as call centres, data entry, medical transcription and online customer support. India is one of the world's biggest IT–ITeS hubs, with cities like **Bengaluru**, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Noida and Gurugram, and the sector is a major source of jobs and exports.

## IT vs ITeS
| IT | ITeS |
| --- | --- |
| Building and maintaining software, hardware and networks | Delivering business services *using* IT |
| Software development, app design, cyber security, cloud | BPO/call centres, data entry, medical transcription, back-office accounting |

- **BPO (Business Process Outsourcing)**: a company hands routine work (customer support, billing, payroll) to another firm.
- **KPO (Knowledge Process Outsourcing)**: outsourcing of knowledge-based work needing expertise – research, data analytics, legal and financial analysis.

## IT applications in daily life
**At home**: online shopping, bill payments, video calls, streaming, smart devices.

**In education**: e-learning platforms (DIKSHA, SWAYAM), smart classes, online tests, digital libraries.

**In banking**: **ATMs**, net banking, mobile banking, **UPI** payments, core banking.

**In healthcare**: electronic health records, **telemedicine** (consulting a doctor online, e.g. eSanjeevani), scanning machines (CT, MRI), online appointments.

**In governance (e-Governance)**: online services for citizens – **DigiLocker** for documents, online tax filing, passport application, Aadhaar-based services, **UMANG** app.

**In transport**: online railway and air ticket booking (IRCTC), GPS navigation, cab apps, **FASTag** toll payment.

**In retail and business**: billing with barcode scanners, inventory management, e-commerce websites.

**In entertainment and communication**: social media, online games, OTT platforms, e-mail.

> [!EXAMPLE] One trip, many IT applications
> You book a train ticket on IRCTC, pay through UPI, get an SMS with your PNR, track the train on an app and show the e-ticket on your phone. That is IT in action at every step.

## New technologies in IT
- **Cloud computing** – storing and using data and software over the Internet.
- **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** – machines that can learn and make decisions (chatbots, voice assistants).
- **Internet of Things (IoT)** – everyday devices connected to the Internet (smart bulbs, smart watches).
- **Big Data** and **data analytics**.
- **Cyber security** – protecting systems from attacks.

## Careers in IT–ITeS
Software developer, web designer, data entry operator, customer care executive, network administrator, data analyst, graphic designer, IT support technician, cyber security analyst.

> [!MISTAKE] IT is not only programming
> Many IT–ITeS jobs need good communication and keyboarding skills rather than coding – e.g. customer care executive or data entry operator.

> [!TIP] Exam tip
> For "applications of IT" questions, write the **field** as a sub-heading and give one specific example (e.g. Banking – ATM, UPI).

## Quick recap
- IT = computers + software + networks for information. ITeS = services delivered using IT.
- BPO handles routine processes; KPO handles knowledge-based work.
- IT is used in home, education, banking, health, governance, transport and retail.
- New trends: cloud, AI, IoT, big data, cyber security.

=== data-entry-and-keyboarding-skills ===
## The big idea
**Data entry** means entering information into a computer accurately and quickly. The **keyboard** is the main tool for this. **Touch typing** – typing without looking at the keys, using all ten fingers – lets you work fast and with fewer errors. It is a key skill for jobs like data entry operator, customer support and office assistant.

## The keyboard layout
Most keyboards use the **QWERTY** layout, named after the first six letters of the top letter row. It was designed by **Christopher Latham Sholes** for typewriters in the 1870s.

| Key group | Keys | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alphanumeric (typing) keys** | Letters A–Z, numbers 0–9, symbols | Typing text |
| **Function keys** | F1–F12 | Special tasks (F1 = Help, F2 = Rename, F5 = Refresh) |
| **Control/modifier keys** | Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Windows key, Esc | Used with other keys for shortcuts |
| **Navigation (cursor) keys** | Arrow keys, Home, End, Page Up, Page Down, Insert, Delete | Move the cursor |
| **Numeric keypad** | 0–9 and +, −, *, / on the right | Fast number entry (turn on Num Lock) |
| **Special keys** | Enter, Backspace, Tab, Caps Lock, Spacebar | Editing and formatting |

## The home row
The **home row** is the middle row of letters, where fingers rest when not typing.
- **Left hand**: little finger on **A**, ring on **S**, middle on **D**, index on **F**.
- **Right hand**: index on **J**, middle on **K**, ring on **L**, little on **;** (semicolon).
- **Thumbs** rest on the **spacebar**.

> [!KEY] Guide keys
> **F** and **J** have small raised bumps. They let you find the home row without looking. Index fingers always return to F and J.

Each finger reaches up or down to keys in its own column (e.g. the left index finger types F, G, R, T, V, B, 4, 5). The **top row** is QWERTYUIOP and the **bottom row** is ZXCVBNM.

## Correct typing technique and posture
- Sit straight with your back supported and feet flat on the floor.
- Keep elbows at about 90°, wrists straight and slightly raised (not resting on the desk while typing).
- Monitor at eye level and about an arm's length away.
- Tap keys lightly; keep eyes on the screen or copy, not on the keyboard.
- Take short breaks; stretch hands and look away from the screen every 20 minutes.

> [!MISTAKE] Speed before accuracy
> Beginners often rush. Focus on **accuracy first**; speed improves naturally with practice. A fast typist with many errors is slower overall because of corrections.

## Measuring typing skill
Typing speed is measured in **WPM (words per minute)**. A "word" is usually counted as **5 characters** (including spaces).
**Gross WPM = (total characters typed ÷ 5) ÷ time in minutes**.
**Accuracy (%) = (correct words ÷ total words) × 100**.

> [!EXAMPLE] Calculate the speed
> You type 1,000 characters in 5 minutes. Words = 1000 ÷ 5 = 200. Speed = 200 ÷ 5 = **40 WPM**.

## Typing tutor software
**Rapid Typing Tutor**, **TuxType**, **KTouch** and **Typing Master** offer lessons (home row, top row, bottom row, numbers), games and tests that show speed and accuracy. Start with home-row lessons, then add one row at a time.

## Data entry tips
- Check the source document carefully; double-check numbers.
- Use the **numeric keypad** for numbers and **Tab** to move between fields.
- Save often (Ctrl+S) and verify entries before submitting.

> [!TIP] Exam tip
> Memorise the home-row finger positions (ASDF – JKL;) and that F and J are guide keys. These appear as MCQs every year.

## Quick recap
- QWERTY layout; key groups: alphanumeric, function, modifier, navigation, numeric keypad, special.
- Home row: A S D F (left) J K L ; (right); F and J are guide keys.
- Good posture: straight back, feet flat, wrists straight, screen at eye level.
- Speed in WPM (5 characters = 1 word); accuracy first.

=== electronic-spreadsheet ===
## The big idea
A **spreadsheet** is a grid of rows and columns used to store, calculate and analyse data – marks, budgets, sales, attendance. **LibreOffice Calc** is a free spreadsheet program. Its files are called **spreadsheets** (workbooks) with the extension **.ods**, and each file contains one or more **sheets** (worksheets).

## The Calc interface
- **Columns** are labelled with letters (A, B, …, Z, AA, AB, …). **Rows** are numbered (1, 2, 3, …).
- A **cell** is where a row and column meet; it is named by its **cell address** or reference, e.g. **B3** (column B, row 3).
- The **active cell** has a dark border; its address shows in the **Name Box**.
- The **Formula bar** (Input line) shows the contents of the active cell.
- A **range** is a group of cells, written as **A1:C5** (from A1 to C5).
- Sheet tabs at the bottom; add a sheet with **Sheet > Insert Sheet** or the **+** button.

## Entering data
Data can be **text (labels)**, **numbers** or **formulas**. Text aligns left, numbers align right by default. Use **AutoFill**: type 1 and 2 in two cells, select both and drag the **fill handle** (small square at the bottom-right) to continue the series. Typing *Monday* and dragging fills the days of the week.

## Formulas and operators
A **formula** always starts with **=**. It can use cell references, so the result updates when data changes.

| Operator | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| + | Addition | =A1+B1 |
| − | Subtraction | =A1−B1 |
| * | Multiplication | =A1*B1 |
| / | Division | =A1/B1 |
| ^ | Exponent (power) | =A1^2 |
| % | Percent | =A1*10% |

Order of calculation follows **BODMAS**: brackets, then ^, then * and /, then + and −. So `=2+3*4` gives **14**, but `=(2+3)*4` gives **20**.

## Functions
Functions are built-in formulas.
| Function | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `=SUM(B2:B6)` | Total of the range |
| `=AVERAGE(B2:B6)` | Mean |
| `=MAX(B2:B6)` / `=MIN(B2:B6)` | Largest / smallest |
| `=COUNT(B2:B6)` | Number of cells with numbers |
| `=ROUND(B2,1)` | Rounds to 1 decimal place |
| `=IF(B2>=33,"Pass","Fail")` | Checks a condition |

> [!EXAMPLE] Report card
> Marks of 5 subjects in B2:F2. Total in G2: `=SUM(B2:F2)`. Percentage in H2: `=G2/500*100`. Result in I2: `=IF(H2>=33,"Pass","Fail")`. Drag G2:I2 down to fill for all students.

## Relative and absolute references
- **Relative** (A1): changes when copied. `=B2*C2` copied down becomes `=B3*C3`.
- **Absolute** ($A$1): does not change when copied – use for a fixed value like a tax rate in E1: `=B2*$E$1`.
- **Mixed** ($A1 or A$1): only one part is fixed. Press **Shift+F4** in Calc to cycle through reference types.

> [!MISTAKE] Forgetting the = sign
> Typing `SUM(A1:A5)` without = shows it as text. Every formula must begin with =.

## Sorting and filtering
- **Sort**: select data → **Data > Sort** → choose the column and **Ascending/Descending**.
- **Filter**: **Data > AutoFilter** adds drop-down arrows to the header row to show only matching rows.

## Charts
Charts show data visually.
1. Select the data (with headings).
2. **Insert > Chart**.
3. Choose a chart type: **Column**, **Bar**, **Pie**, **Line**, **Area**, **XY (Scatter)**.
4. Set data range, data series, titles and legend in the wizard → **Finish**.

Use a **pie chart** for parts of a whole, a **column/bar chart** to compare values, and a **line chart** to show change over time.

> [!TIP] Common exam trap
> A question may ask the output of `=10+20/2*3`. Do division and multiplication left to right first: 20/2 = 10, × 3 = 30, + 10 = **40**.

## Quick recap
- Calc file: .ods; cell address = column letter + row number; range A1:C5.
- Formulas begin with =; BODMAS order; functions SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT, IF.
- Relative refs change when copied; absolute ($A$1) do not.
- Data > Sort, Data > AutoFilter; Insert > Chart (column, bar, pie, line).

=== digital-presentation ===
## The big idea
A **presentation** is a set of **slides** used to share ideas with an audience, using text, images, charts, sound and video. **LibreOffice Impress** is a free presentation program; its files have the extension **.odp** (OpenDocument Presentation). Good presentations are short, visual and easy to read.

## Starting Impress
Open **Applications > Office > LibreOffice Impress**. Choose a template from **Select a Template** or close it to start with a blank presentation. The window shows:
- **Slide pane** (thumbnails of slides) on the left,
- **Workspace** in the middle (the current slide),
- **Sidebar** on the right (Properties, Slide Transition, Animation, Master Slides…),
- Menus, toolbars and status bar.

## Working with slides
- **New slide**: **Slide > New Slide** (Ctrl+M), or right-click in the Slide pane → New Slide.
- **Duplicate**: **Slide > Duplicate Slide**.

- **Rename**: right-click → **Rename Slide**.
- **Move**: drag the slide in the Slide pane or in Slide Sorter view.

## Slide layouts
A **layout** is the arrangement of placeholders (title, content, pictures) on a slide. Choose it from **Slide > Layout** or the **Layouts** panel in the **Properties** sidebar. Common layouts: **Blank Slide**, **Title Slide**, **Title, Content**, **Title and 2 Content**, **Title Only**, **Centred Text**.

| View | Use |

| **Normal** | Main view to create and edit slides |
| **Outline** | Shows titles and bullet points of all slides as text |
| **Notes** | Add speaker notes below each slide |
| **Slide Sorter** | Thumbnails of all slides; rearrange, delete, apply transitions |
| **Handout** | Arrange several slides per page for printing |

Change views using the **View** menu.

## Adding content
- Click a placeholder to type; use the Formatting toolbar for font, size, colour and bullets.
- **Insert > Image** for pictures; **Insert > Chart**; **Insert > Table**; **Insert > Audio or Video**; **Insert > Text Box** (F2).
- **Master slide** (View > Master Slide) sets common design (logo, font, background) for all slides. **Slide > Slide Properties** changes background or page size.

## Transitions and animations
- **Transition**: effect *between* slides. Select slides → **Slide > Slide Transition** (or the Slide Transition sidebar panel) → choose effect (Wipe, Fade, Push…), variant, duration and sound; *Advance slide*: On mouse click or Automatically after N seconds; **Apply Transition to All Slides** if needed.
- **Animation**: effect on an *object* within a slide. Select the object → **Format > Animation** (or the Animation sidebar) → **+ Add** → choose Entrance, Emphasis, Exit or Motion Path effect, and its start (On click, With previous, After previous).

> [!MISTAKE] Transition vs animation
> **Transition** applies to the whole slide as it changes to the next. **Animation** applies to text or objects inside a slide.

## Running the slide show
- **F5**: start from the first slide (**Slide Show > Start from First Slide**).
- **Shift+F5**: start from the current slide.
- Move with **Enter, Spacebar, arrow keys, Page Down** or mouse click; **Esc** ends the show.
- **Slide Show > Slide Show Settings** sets range, loop, pointer options.

## Tips for an effective presentation
- One main idea per slide; about **6 bullet points with 6 words each** (the 6×6 rule).
- Large, readable fonts (at least 24 pt for text); good contrast.
- Use images and charts instead of long paragraphs.
- Keep transitions and animations simple and consistent.

> [!EXAMPLE] A 5-slide project on "Save Water"
> 1. Title Slide – title and your name. 2. Why water is precious – 3 bullets and a picture. 3. Causes of water waste – Title, Content. 4. How we can save water – Title and 2 Content (text + image). 5. Thank you – Centred Text. Apply a Fade transition to all and start with F5.

> [!TIP] Shortcut marks
> Ctrl+M new slide, F5 start show, Shift+F5 from current slide, Esc end show.

## Quick recap
- Impress files: .odp; a presentation is a set of slides.
- Layouts arrange placeholders; views: Normal, Outline, Notes, Slide Sorter, Handout.
- Transition = between slides; animation = objects within a slide.
- F5 start, Shift+F5 current slide, Esc exit; keep slides simple.
