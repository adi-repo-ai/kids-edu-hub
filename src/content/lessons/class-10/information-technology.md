=== communication-skills ===
## The big idea
**Communication** is the process of sharing information, ideas, feelings or instructions between two or more people so that the message is understood. At work, good communication gets jobs done right the first time; poor communication causes errors, delays and conflicts. In Class 10 you revise the methods and cycle of communication, and learn what blocks it and how to remove those blocks.

> [!KEY] Communication cycle
> **Sender → Message (encoded) → Channel → Receiver (decodes) → Feedback** back to the sender. Communication is complete only when feedback shows the message was understood.

## Methods of communication
| Method | What it uses | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verbal** – oral | Spoken words | Face-to-face talk, phone call, video meeting |
| **Verbal** – written | Written words | Letter, e-mail, SMS, notice, report |
| **Non-verbal** | Body language and voice | Facial expressions, gestures, posture, eye contact, touch, personal space, tone and pitch (paralanguage) |
| **Visual** | Pictures and symbols | Traffic signs, charts, maps, emojis, logos |

Studies of face-to-face conversations suggest that a large share of meaning comes from **tone and body language**, not just words. So keep your posture open, make eye contact, nod to show you are listening, and keep your tone friendly.

> [!EXAMPLE] Non-verbal cues at an interview
> Positive: sitting upright, a firm handshake (where appropriate), smiling, nodding. Negative: crossed arms, slouching, looking at the phone, tapping fingers.

## The 7 Cs of effective communication
A message is effective when it is **Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete and Courteous**.
- **Clear** – one idea at a time, simple words.
- **Concise** – no unnecessary words.
- **Concrete** – specific facts and figures, not vague ideas.
- **Correct** – accurate facts, grammar and spelling.
- **Coherent** – logical flow, ideas connected.
- **Complete** – all the information the receiver needs.
- **Courteous** – polite and respectful.

## Barriers to effective communication
| Barrier | Meaning | Example / remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Physical** | Noise, distance, poor network, bad lighting | Move to a quiet room; check the connection |
| **Linguistic (language)** | Different language, jargon, unclear words | Use simple words; avoid jargon |
| **Interpersonal** | Lack of trust, attitude, emotions between people | Listen with respect; stay calm |
| **Organisational** | Too many levels, strict rules, poor systems | Clear reporting lines, open-door policy |
| **Cultural** | Different customs, values, gestures | Learn and respect other cultures |
| **Psychological** | Stress, anger, fear, low attention | Speak when calm; be patient |

> [!MISTAKE] Noise is not only sound
> In communication, **noise** means anything that disturbs the message: a crackling phone line, a confusing word or even the listener's worry.

## Principles to overcome barriers
- Use simple language and check understanding (ask "Is that clear?").
- **Active listening**: pay full attention, do not interrupt, ask questions, summarise.
- Be respectful and keep emotions under control.
- Choose the right channel: urgent matter → phone; record needed → e-mail or letter.
- Give and accept **feedback** positively.

## Writing skills (quick revision)
Good writing needs correct **parts of speech** (noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection), complete **sentences** (subject + verb + object) and well-organised **paragraphs** (one main idea, a topic sentence, supporting sentences). Sentences can be statements, questions, exclamations or commands; prefer the **active voice** in workplace writing.

> [!TIP] Exam pattern
> 2-mark questions often ask you to "name any four barriers" or "list the 7 Cs". Write the term in bold and add a one-line example for full marks.

## Quick recap
- Communication cycle: sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback.
- Methods: verbal (oral/written), non-verbal, visual.
- 7 Cs: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
- Barriers: physical, linguistic, interpersonal, organisational, cultural, psychological.
- Active listening and feedback remove most barriers.

=== self-management-skills ===
## The big idea
**Self-management** means taking responsibility for your own behaviour, feelings, time and well-being. Employers value people who can manage stress, stay motivated without constant supervision, set goals and use time well. This chapter covers **stress management**, **self-awareness**, **self-motivation**, **goal setting** and **time management**.

## Stress and stress management
**Stress** is the body's and mind's reaction to pressure or a demanding situation. A little stress can push us to perform, but too much harms health, sleep and studies.

Common causes for students: exams, peer pressure, family expectations, too many tasks, conflicts and illness.

> [!KEY] Stress management techniques
> - **Time management** – plan and prioritise work so it does not pile up.
> - **Physical exercise and yoga** – release tension and improve mood.
> - **Meditation and deep breathing** – calm the mind.
> - **Healthy diet and enough sleep** (8 hours for teenagers is a good target).
> - **Positive thinking** and talking to family, friends or a counsellor.
> - **Taking breaks and hobbies** – music, sports, nature walks, holidays.

> [!MISTAKE] Avoiding the task is not stress management
> Postponing work feels good for a moment, but the pending task increases stress later. Break it into small parts and start.

## Self-awareness
**Self-awareness** is knowing your own strengths, weaknesses, interests, values and emotions. It is the first step to self-improvement.
- **Strengths**: what you are good at (e.g. drawing, maths, explaining things).
- **Weaknesses**: areas to improve (e.g. stage fear, handwriting).
You can find them by self-reflection, feedback from teachers and friends, and by noticing which activities energise you.

## Self-motivation
**Motivation** is the inner push that makes us act. **Self-motivation** means pushing ourselves without needing others to remind us.

| Type | Source | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal (intrinsic)** | Inner joy, interest, satisfaction | Reading because you love stories |
| **External (extrinsic)** | Rewards, marks, praise, money, fear of punishment | Studying for a prize |

Qualities of self-motivated people: they know what they want, are focused, stay positive after failure, and keep learning.

> [!TIP] Build self-motivation
> Find what you truly enjoy, set small goals, celebrate small wins, and keep reminding yourself *why* the goal matters to you.

## Goal setting: SMART goals
A goal should be **SMART**:
- **S**pecific – clear and exact ("score 90 in IT", not "do well").
- **M**easurable – progress can be checked.
- **A**chievable – realistic for you.
- **R**ealistic / Relevant – fits your bigger aims.
- **T**ime-bound – has a deadline.

> [!EXAMPLE] Turning a wish into a SMART goal
> Wish: "I want to type fast." SMART goal: "I will practise typing for 20 minutes daily using a typing tutor and reach 30 words per minute by 31 December."

## Time management
Four steps help you manage time:
1. **Organise** – make a list or timetable of tasks.
2. **Prioritise** – do important and urgent tasks first.
3. **Control** – avoid distractions like social media during study time.
4. **Track** – check at the end of the day what got done and adjust.

## Quick recap
- Stress management: time planning, exercise, yoga, meditation, sleep, diet, positive thinking, hobbies.
- Self-awareness = knowing strengths and weaknesses.
- Motivation can be internal (intrinsic) or external (extrinsic).
- Goals should be SMART; time management = organise, prioritise, control, track.

=== ict-skills ===
## The big idea
**ICT (Information and Communication Technology)** skills are the everyday computer skills every job now needs: understanding the parts of a computer, using the operating system, managing files, keeping the computer healthy and secure, and using office tools like spreadsheets and presentations.

## Computer system and software
A computer system has **hardware** (physical parts – CPU, keyboard, monitor, printer) and **software** (programs).

| Software type | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **System software** | Runs the hardware and other programs | Operating systems – Windows, Linux (Ubuntu), macOS, Android |
| **Utility software** | Maintains and protects the computer | Antivirus, disk cleanup, backup, compression tools |
| **Application software** | Does user tasks | LibreOffice Writer/Calc/Impress, browsers, media players |

## Operating system and user interface
The **operating system (OS)** is the link between the user, applications and hardware. It manages memory, files, processes and devices, and provides a **user interface**:
- **CUI/CLI (Character/Command Line)** – type commands (e.g. MS-DOS, Linux terminal).
- **GUI (Graphical)** – icons, windows, menus and a mouse (Windows, Ubuntu desktop).
- **Touch, gesture and voice-based** interfaces – smartphones, smart speakers.

## Files and folders
A **file** stores data (a document, picture, song); its **extension** shows the type: `.odt` (Writer), `.ods` (Calc), `.odp` (Impress), `.odb` (Base), `.jpg`, `.mp3`. Files are kept in **folders (directories)**.

> [!EXAMPLE] Common file actions in Ubuntu/Windows
> - New folder: right-click empty space → **New Folder**.
> - Rename: select the file → **F2** → type the new name → Enter.
> - Copy/Move: **Ctrl+C** / **Ctrl+X**, then **Ctrl+V** in the new folder.
> - Delete: select → **Delete** key (it goes to Recycle Bin/Trash; **Shift+Delete** deletes permanently).

## Computer care and maintenance
- **Clean** keyboard, screen and vents regularly; keep food and liquids away.
- Run **Disk Cleanup** and **defragmentation** (for hard disks), delete unwanted files.
- Keep the OS and software **updated**.
- Take regular **backups** on an external drive or cloud.
- Scan with **antivirus**; do not open unknown attachments.

## Computer security and privacy
Threats you must know:
- **Virus** – attaches itself to files and spreads when they are opened.
- **Worm** – spreads by itself through networks.
- **Trojan horse** – looks useful but secretly harms the system.
- **Spyware** – secretly collects your data; **adware** shows unwanted ads.
- **Phishing** – fake e-mails/websites that trick you into giving passwords or bank details.

Protection: antivirus, **firewall**, strong passwords, updates, avoiding unknown links, logging out of public computers, using only secure (**https**) sites for payments.

> [!MISTAKE] A worm is not a virus
> A virus needs a host file and a user action to spread; a worm spreads on its own over the network.

## Spreadsheet and presentation basics
- **Spreadsheet (LibreOffice Calc)**: data in **cells** named by column letter + row number (B3). Formulas start with `=`, e.g. `=SUM(B2:B10)`, `=AVERAGE(C2:C6)`. Insert a chart: select data → **Insert > Chart**.
- **Presentation (LibreOffice Impress)**: new slide **Slide > New Slide** (Ctrl+M); choose a layout from the **Layouts** panel of the sidebar; add transitions in the **Slide Transition** panel; start the show with **F5**.

> [!TIP] Keyboard shortcuts score easy marks
> Ctrl+S save, Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, Ctrl+A select all, Ctrl+P print, F5 start slide show.

## Quick recap
- Software: system, utility, application. OS manages hardware and gives a user interface.
- Interfaces: CUI, GUI, touch, voice, gesture.
- Maintain by cleaning, updating, backing up and scanning.
- Threats: virus, worm, trojan, spyware, phishing; protect with antivirus, firewall, strong passwords.

=== entrepreneurial-skills ===
## The big idea
An **entrepreneur** is a person who sees an opportunity, takes a **risk**, and starts a business (an **enterprise**) to solve a problem or meet a need. **Entrepreneurship** is the process of doing so. Entrepreneurs create jobs, bring new products and help the economy grow.

## Entrepreneurship and society
Entrepreneurs help society by:
- **creating employment** for others;
- **bringing new products and services** that make life easier;
- **using local resources and skills** (e.g. handicrafts, food products);
- **increasing the country's income** and reducing poverty;
- **solving social problems** – affordable sanitary pads, clean drinking water, low-cost solar lights.

> [!EXAMPLE] Local to large
> A tailor who starts a small boutique, a farmer who sells organic vegetables online, or a student who starts a tiffin service are all entrepreneurs at different scales.

## Qualities of a successful entrepreneur
| Quality | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Patience** | Business takes time to succeed |
| **Positivity** | Sees opportunity in problems |
| **Hard work** | Ready to work long hours |
| **Confidence** | Believes in the idea and in self |
| **Creativity / innovation** | Thinks of new ideas and new ways |
| **Risk-taking** | Ready to invest time and money despite uncertainty |
| **Decision-making** | Takes timely decisions |
| **Perseverance** | Does not give up after failure |
| **Open to learning** | Learns from customers and mistakes |

## Functions of an entrepreneur
1. **Making decisions** – what to sell, where, at what price.
2. **Managing the business** – arranging money (finance), people and materials.
3. **Dividing income** – paying workers, suppliers, rent, and keeping profit.
4. **Taking risks** – bearing possible losses.
5. **Creating demand** – advertising and marketing.
6. **Fulfilling customer needs** – quality products and good service.

## Myths about entrepreneurship
> [!MISTAKE] Common myths
> - *"Only rich people can start a business."* – Many start small with savings or loans (e.g. MUDRA loans) and grow.
> - *"Entrepreneurs are born, not made."* – Skills can be learned through training and practice.
> - *"You need a totally new idea."* – Improving an existing product or service also works.
> - *"Entrepreneurs never fail."* – Most face failures and learn from them.

## Entrepreneurship as a career option
Like any job, being an entrepreneur is a **career choice**. The career process usually goes: **Start-up → Growth → Expansion** (and maybe diversification). It gives independence, flexibility and the chance to earn more, but also carries risk and uncertainty.

## Types of business (revision)
- **Product business** – makes/sells goods (bakery, clothing).
- **Service business** – provides a service (salon, repair shop, tuition).
- **Hybrid business** – both (a restaurant makes food and serves it).

> [!TIP] Answer with an Indian example
> In long answers, add an example such as a local dairy, a handicraft shop or a famous start-up to show understanding.

## Quick recap
- Entrepreneur = risk-taker who starts an enterprise; entrepreneurship = the process.
- Society gains jobs, products, income and solutions.
- Qualities: patience, positivity, hard work, confidence, creativity, risk-taking, decision-making.
- Functions: decisions, managing, dividing income, risk, creating demand, meeting customer needs.
- Entrepreneurship is a learnable career option.

=== green-skills ===
## The big idea
**Green skills** are the knowledge, abilities and attitudes needed to live and work in a way that protects the environment. In Class 10 the focus is **sustainable development** – progress that does not use up the planet.

> [!KEY] Sustainable development
> Development that **meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs** (Brundtland Commission report, *Our Common Future*, 1987).

## Why do we need it?
The world's population and consumption keep rising, but natural resources are limited. Problems we face:
- **Food** – loss of fertile soil, overuse of chemical fertilisers and pesticides.
- **Water** – falling groundwater, polluted rivers.
- **Energy** – dependence on coal and oil, which cause pollution and global warming.
- **Biodiversity loss** – deforestation and habitat destruction.
- **Waste** – plastic and e-waste piling up.

## The three pillars
Sustainable development balances three things:
1. **Environment** – protect air, water, soil, forests and wildlife.
2. **Society** – health, education, equality and jobs for all.
3. **Economy** – growth and income, without destroying resources.

## Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
In **2015**, the **United Nations** adopted **17 Sustainable Development Goals** to be achieved by **2030**. India is committed to them. Some of the goals:

| No. | Goal |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | No poverty |
| 2 | Zero hunger |
| 3 | Good health and well-being |
| 4 | Quality education |
| 5 | Gender equality |
| 6 | Clean water and sanitation |
| 7 | Affordable and clean energy |
| 13 | Climate action |
| 14 | Life below water |
| 15 | Life on land |
| 17 | Partnerships for the goals |

> [!MISTAKE] Get the numbers right
> There are **17** SDGs, adopted in **2015**, with a target year of **2030**. Do not confuse them with the 8 Millennium Development Goals (2000–2015).

## Our role in sustainable development
- **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle** (and Refuse single-use plastic).
- Save electricity: switch off lights and fans, use LED bulbs and star-rated appliances.
- Save water: fix leaks, harvest rainwater, reuse RO waste water.
- Use public transport, cycle or walk; car-pool.
- Plant trees and avoid wasting food and paper.
- Segregate waste into **wet (green bin)** and **dry (blue bin)**; dispose of e-waste at authorised centres.
- Prefer solar energy and other **renewable** sources.

## Green jobs
Jobs that help protect or restore the environment are **green jobs** – solar panel technician, organic farmer, waste management worker, water conservation expert, eco-tourism guide.

> [!TIP] Easy marks
> When asked "how can students contribute", write specific actions (switching off fans, carrying a cloth bag) rather than vague lines like "save the earth".

## Quick recap
- Sustainable development: meet present needs without harming future generations (Brundtland, 1987).
- Three pillars: environment, society, economy.
- 17 SDGs adopted by the UN in 2015, target 2030.
- Personal actions: 3Rs, saving water and energy, public transport, waste segregation.

=== digital-documentation-advanced ===
## The big idea
In Class 9 you learnt to type and format in **LibreOffice Writer**. In Class 10 you learn the power tools that make long, professional documents easy: **styles**, **images and drawing objects**, **templates**, an automatic **table of contents**, and **mail merge** for sending personalised letters to many people.

## Styles
A **style** is a saved set of formatting (font, size, colour, spacing, indent) with a name. Change the style once and every part using it updates. Open the **Styles** deck with **View > Styles** or **F11**.

LibreOffice has these style categories:
| Category | Applies to |
| --- | --- |
| **Paragraph** | Whole paragraphs – headings, body text |
| **Character** | Selected words or letters |
| **Frame** | Frames and images |
| **Page** | Margins, size, headers/footers, orientation |
| **List** | Bullets and numbering |
| **Table** | Table formatting |

Ways to apply a style: double-click its name in the Styles deck; use the **Set Paragraph Style** box on the Formatting toolbar; use **Fill Format Mode** (the paint-can icon) to apply a style by clicking; or shortcuts (**Ctrl+1** Heading 1, **Ctrl+2** Heading 2, **Ctrl+0** Body Text).

> [!EXAMPLE] Create a new style from selection
> 1. Format a paragraph the way you want (e.g. Arial 14, blue).
> 2. In the Styles deck, click the **Styles actions** menu → **New Style from Selection**.
> 3. Type a name, e.g. *MyHeading*, and click **OK**.
> To change it later, right-click the style → **Modify**. To match a style to edited text, use **Update Selected Style**. **Load Styles** copies styles from another document or template.

## Images and drawing objects
Insert a picture with **Insert > Image**, choose the file, click **Open**. You can also drag and drop, copy-paste, or scan (**Insert > Media > Scan**). Right-click an image to:
- **Crop** – remove unwanted edges (**Format > Image > Properties > Crop** tab).

## The big idea
**LibreOffice Calc** can do far more than add numbers. In Class 10 you learn to **combine data** from many sheets (consolidate), **summarise** data with subtotals, ask **"what if?"** questions with scenarios, goal seek and solver, **link** sheets and files, **share and track changes**, and automate repeated work with **macros**.

## Consolidating data
**Consolidation** combines data from several ranges (often on different sheets) into one summary.
1. Open the sheet where the result should go.
2. **Data > Consolidate**.
3. Choose a **Function** (Sum, Count, Average, Max, Min, Product, Count Numbers, StDev, Var…).
4. In **Source data ranges**, select each range and click **Add**.
5. In **Copy results to**, select the target cell.
6. Under **Options**, tick **Row labels / Column labels** if needed and **Link to source data** so the result updates when the source changes. Click **OK**.

## Subtotals
**Data > Subtotals** inserts sub-totals for groups of rows.
1. **Sort** the data first by the column you will group on (Data > Sort).
2. Select the data → **Data > Subtotals**.
3. In the **1st Group** tab: *Group by* (e.g. Region), *Calculate subtotals for* (e.g. Sales), *Use function* (Sum, Average, Count…).
4. Click **OK**. Calc shows outline buttons (1, 2, 3) on the left to show/hide detail.

> [!MISTAKE] Sort before subtotals
> If the grouping column is not sorted, the same group appears in many places and you get many small, wrong subtotals.

## What-if analysis
| Tool | Menu | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scenarios** | **Tools > Scenarios** | Save different sets of input values (e.g. best/likely/worst case) and switch between them using the Navigator |
| **Goal Seek** | **Tools > Goal Seek** | Find the **one input** needed to reach a desired result |
| **Solver** | **Tools > Solver** | Find the best values of **several inputs** with constraints (maximise/minimise) |
| **Multiple Operations** | **Data > Multiple Operations** | Build a table showing results for many input values |

> [!EXAMPLE] Goal Seek
> A student has 70, 80 and 85 in three tests (B1:B3) and the fourth test in B4. Average in B5: `=AVERAGE(B1:B4)`. What score in B4 gives an average of 80?
> **Tools > Goal Seek** → Formula cell: B5, Target value: 80, Variable cell: B4 → OK. Calc answers **85**.

## Linking data
- Reference another sheet: `=Sheet2.B4` (Calc uses a **dot**; with quotes for names with spaces: `='Term 1'.B4`). Or type `=`, click the other sheet's cell, press Enter.
- Reference another file: `='file:///home/user/marks.ods'#$Sheet1.A1`.
- **Hyperlinks**: **Insert > Hyperlink** (Ctrl+K) to link to a website, file, or a cell/sheet in the same document.
- **Sheet > Link to External Data** brings in a table from a web page or another file.

## Sharing and reviewing
- **Share**: **Tools > Share Spreadsheet** – tick *Share this spreadsheet with other users*, so several people can edit it.
- **Record changes**: **Edit > Track Changes > Record** (Ctrl+Shift+C); changes are highlighted with author and time.
- **Accept/reject**: **Edit > Track Changes > Manage**.
- **Comments**: **Insert > Comment** (Ctrl+Alt+C).
- **Compare**: **Edit > Track Changes > Compare Document**; **Merge Document** combines copies.

## Macros
A **macro** is a saved sequence of commands or keystrokes that can be replayed to automate repetitive tasks.
1. Enable recording: **Tools > Options > LibreOffice > Advanced > Enable macro recording**.
2. **Tools > Macros > Record Macro**, do the actions, click **Stop Recording**.
3. Choose a library/module (e.g. in **Standard**), give a name, **Save**.
4. Run: **Tools > Macros > Run Macro**, select it, **Run**.

Macros are written in **LibreOffice Basic** and can also be used as custom functions in formulas.

> [!TIP] One-input vs many-input
> Goal Seek = one variable, one target. Solver = many variables with constraints. Examiners love this difference.

=== database-management-system ===
## The big idea
A **database** is an organised collection of related data, like a school's record of students, marks and fees. A **DBMS (Database Management System)** is software to create, store, update and retrieve that data efficiently. You will use **LibreOffice Base**, which stores a whole database in one **.odb** file using its built-in **HSQLDB** engine.

## Data, information and DBMS
- **Data** = raw facts (e.g. 85, "Riya"). **Information** = processed, meaningful data ("Riya scored 85 in IT").
- Examples of DBMS: MySQL, Oracle, Microsoft Access, LibreOffice Base, PostgreSQL.
- **Advantages**: less **data redundancy** (duplication), less **inconsistency**, data sharing, data security, data integrity, backup and recovery.

## Relational database terms
In a **relational database (RDBMS)** data is stored in **tables (relations)**.
| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Field / attribute / column** | One piece of information, e.g. RollNo, Name |
| **Record / tuple / row** | All information about one item, e.g. one student |
| **Primary key** | Field that uniquely identifies each record; cannot be empty or repeated (e.g. AdmissionNo) |
| **Composite key** | Primary key made of two or more fields together |
| **Candidate key** | Any field(s) that could serve as primary key |
| **Alternate key** | A candidate key not chosen as primary key |
| **Foreign key** | Field in one table that refers to the primary key of another table, linking them |

> [!MISTAKE] Name is a poor primary key
> Two students can share a name. Choose a field that is always unique, like admission number.

## Data types in Base
- **Numeric**: Boolean (Yes/No), Tinyint, Smallint, Integer, BigInt, Numeric, Decimal, Real, Float, Double.
- **Alphanumeric/text**: Text [VARCHAR], Text (fix) [CHAR], Memo [LONGVARCHAR].
- **Date/Time**: Date, Time, Date/Time (Timestamp).
- **Binary**: Image [LONGVARBINARY] for photos.

## Creating a database and table
1. **File > New > Database** → *Create a new database* (Embedded HSQLDB) → Next → *Yes, register the database* → Finish → name it (e.g. School.odb) → Save.
2. In **Tables**, click **Create Table in Design View**.
3. Type **Field Names**, choose **Field Type**, set **Field Properties** (length, default, required).
4. Right-click the row selector of the unique field → **Primary Key**.
5. Save (Ctrl+S) and name the table. Double-click the table to enter records (**Datasheet view**).

You can also use **Use Wizard to Create Table**. Sort with the Sort buttons and filter with AutoFilter in datasheet view.

## Relationships
**Tools > Relationships** – add tables and drag a primary key to the matching foreign key.
- **One-to-one** – one record matches exactly one (student ↔ ID card).
- **One-to-many** – one record matches many (one class has many students). Most common.
- **Many-to-many** – many match many (students ↔ clubs), handled through a linking table.
Relationships maintain **referential integrity** (you cannot enter a foreign key value that has no matching primary key).

## Queries
A **query** asks a question of the database and shows matching records. In Base, under **Queries**: **Create Query in Design View**, **Use Wizard to Create Query**, or **Create Query in SQL View**.

> [!EXAMPLE] SQL query
> `SELECT Name, Marks FROM Student WHERE Marks > 80 ORDER BY Marks DESC;`
> Shows names and marks of students scoring above 80, highest first. In Design View you would add the table, tick the fields, type `>80` in the **Criterion** row and choose *descending* in **Sort**.

SQL commands: **DDL** (CREATE, ALTER, DROP – define structure) and **DML** (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE – work with data).

## Forms and reports
- **Form** – a friendly screen for entering and viewing data one record at a time. **Forms > Use Wizard to Create Form**: choose table, fields, arrangement (columnar, tabular…), style; finish.
- **Report** – formatted, printable output of data, often with grouping and totals. **Reports > Use Wizard to Create Report**: choose fields, labels, grouping, sort order, layout. Reports can be **static** or **dynamic** (updated each time they open).

> [!TIP] Form vs report
> Form = **input** (and view). Report = **output** for printing/summary.

## Quick recap
- DBMS reduces redundancy and inconsistency; Base files are .odb.
- Table = rows (records) × columns (fields). Primary key is unique and not null; foreign key links tables.
- Tools > Relationships: 1:1, 1:many, many:many.
- Queries retrieve data (Design view / Wizard / SQL). Forms enter data; reports present it.

=== web-applications-and-security ===
## The big idea
This unit covers three things every computer user should know: **accessibility options** that make computers usable by everyone, **networking and the Internet** that connect us, and **online safety and security** that keep us protected.

## Accessibility options
**Accessibility** means designing computers so people with disabilities (vision, hearing, movement, learning) can use them. In Windows: **Control Panel > Ease of Access > Ease of Access Center** (newer Windows: **Settings > Accessibility**).

| Need | Option | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard | **StickyKeys** | Press Shift, Ctrl, Alt one at a time instead of together |
| Keyboard | **FilterKeys** | Ignores brief or repeated keystrokes |
| Keyboard | **ToggleKeys** | Plays a sound when Caps Lock, Num Lock or Scroll Lock is pressed |
| Sound | **SoundSentry** | Shows a visual warning (flash) in place of sounds |
| Sound | **ShowSounds** | Displays captions for speech and sounds |
| Display | **High Contrast** | Uses strong contrasting colours for readability |
| Display | **Cursor options** | Changes cursor blink rate and width |
| Mouse | **MouseKeys** | Moves the pointer using the numeric keypad |
| Others | **Magnifier, Narrator, On-Screen Keyboard** | Zoom screen, read text aloud, type with a pointer |

**Serial Keys** let alternative input devices be attached through a serial port.

## Networking basics
A **network** is two or more computers connected to share data and resources (printers, Internet).
- **Peer-to-peer (P2P)**: all computers are equal; each can share. Good for small setups.
- **Client-server**: a central **server** provides services to **clients**. Secure and manageable for large setups.

Types by area:
- **PAN** – personal (Bluetooth between phone and earbuds).
- **LAN** – a room, building or campus.
- **MAN** – a city (cable TV network).
- **WAN** – countries/continents; the **Internet** is the largest WAN.

The **Internet** is a global network of networks. The **World Wide Web (WWW)** is a system of linked web pages accessed through the Internet using a browser. An **intranet** is a private network for an organisation; an **extranet** allows limited outside access.

Internet access options: dial-up (slow, uses phone line), **DSL**, **cable**, **satellite**, **3G/4G/5G mobile**, **Wi-Fi** (wireless LAN through a router/hotspot), **WiMAX**. Data travels in small **packets**, each carrying source and destination **IP addresses**.

## Using web applications
- **Instant messaging** – real-time text chat (e.g. WhatsApp, Google Chat).
- **Blogs** – online journals with posts in reverse date order (Blogger, WordPress).
- **Online transactions** – shopping, banking, UPI payments; check for **https** and a padlock.
- **E-mail, cloud storage, online office tools**.

## Online safety and security
Threats: **viruses, worms, trojans, spyware, phishing, identity theft, cyberbullying**.

> [!KEY] Safe habits
> - Use **strong passwords**: at least 8 characters, mix of upper and lower case letters, numbers and symbols; do not use names or birthdays; do not share.
> - Use **antivirus** and keep it updated; turn on the **firewall** (blocks unauthorised network access).
> - Do not click unknown links or download from untrusted sites.
> - **Clear browsing history and cookies**, especially on shared computers; use private browsing.
> - Log out after use; avoid saving passwords on public computers.
> - Share minimum personal information on social media.

**Cookies** are small text files a website stores on your computer to remember you; they can be misused to track you.

> [!MISTAKE] Strong does not mean long only
> "aaaaaaaaaa" is long but weak. A strong password mixes character types, e.g. `Mn#47kuR!q`.

## Occupational health and safety
Workplaces must reduce hazards: **ergonomics** (good chair, screen at eye level, regular breaks), **electrical safety**, **fire safety** (know exits; use the correct fire extinguisher), and **first aid**. Basic emergency steps: stay calm, raise the alarm, evacuate by stairs (not lifts), and report the accident.

> [!TIP] Common 1-mark questions
> "Which feature plays a sound when Caps Lock is pressed?" → ToggleKeys. "Which shows visual warnings for sounds?" → SoundSentry.

## Quick recap
- Accessibility: StickyKeys, FilterKeys, ToggleKeys, SoundSentry, ShowSounds, High Contrast, MouseKeys.
- Networks: P2P vs client-server; PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN; Internet ≠ WWW.
- Web apps: IM, blogs, online transactions.
- Safety: strong passwords, antivirus, firewall, clear cookies, think before clicking.
